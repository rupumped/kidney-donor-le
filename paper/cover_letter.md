Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Life Expectancy Changes Due to Kidney Donation Across Race, Sex, and Age," for publication as an Original Article in *Medical Decision Making*.

Living kidney donors face a small but well-documented increase in long-term risk of end-stage renal disease. Yet donors are counseled with relative risks and cumulative incidences rather than with a decision-relevant measure of what donation costs them. Using a Markov model parameterized from US registry data and published donor cohorts, this manuscript translates that risk into life expectancy stratified by age, race, and sex at donation. It also estimates the value of the priority-access mechanisms that policy offers donors and their families. The result depends chiefly on a single unresolved question in the literature, the time-course of donor all-cause mortality risk, and the manuscript quantifies what is at stake in resolving it. The work suits *Medical Decision Making* because it addresses informed consent for a preference-sensitive decision and uses structural sensitivity analysis to show which evidence gap matters most.

The manuscript is under consideration only at *Medical Decision Making* and has not been published previously, in whole or in part. **[TO CONFIRM: disclose any preprint posting, or delete this note.]** The analysis uses only published, publicly available aggregate data from the United States Renal Data System, the Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients, and published cohort studies, including those of Muzaale and colleagues.
