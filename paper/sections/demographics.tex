\section*{Demographic Classification Variables}
\label{sec:demographics}
Race is used in this analysis as reported in the underlying data sources rather than as a directly measured biological quantity. Donor-side race data~\cite{muzaale_risk_2014,massie_quantifying_2017,srtr_2023,usrds_2024,usrds_2025} derive from categories reported to the Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network (OPTN) at donor evaluation and registration; the non-donor comparator population used by Muzaale et al.~\cite{muzaale_risk_2014} was drawn from the Third National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES~III), in which race is self-reported by participants. Race is included in the model because it is the variable available in these source populations and because it is strongly associated with measured post-donation ESRD incidence. Part of this association has an identified biological correlate, \textit{APOL1} renal-risk-variant genotypes, which are more common among individuals of recent African ancestry and are directly implicated in donor kidney-disease risk.~\cite{blackfolks} However, socioeconomic status, healthcare access, comorbidity burden, and other factors correlated with self-reported race in the United States were not available in the source registries and are not separately modeled here. The race-stratified life-expectancy estimates reported in this paper should therefore be interpreted as reflecting the net effect of these unmeasured, race-correlated factors together with any effect of race itself, rather than race operating as an isolated causal variable.
